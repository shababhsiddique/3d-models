# Shelf Phone Scanner Mount

A one-piece 3D-printed mount that clamps onto the front edge of a 20 mm shelf and holds a phone
screen-up in a tray below the shelf, with its rear cameras looking down through a window in the tray
floor (document / object scanning). Three M4 screws, turned from the top with printed thumb caps, push
a captive pressure pad down onto the shelf; TPU liners on both jaws grip the shelf without marking it.

Modelled in **ScanRuler** (scanruler.com, Reverse engineer workspace) through the `scanruler` MCP
server (`npx -y scanruler-mcp@latest`). The model is fully parametric: change a parameter, rebuild,
re-export.

## Coordinate frame

- **X** along the shelf, left to right. X = 0 is the flat left end - the print face (clamp, posts and tray all end flush there).
- **Y** into the shelf. Y = 0 is the shelf front face = inner face of the clamp spine.
- **Z** up. Z = 0 is the shelf underside.

## Design summary

**Clamp.** C-profile sketched on the YZ plane, extruded X 0..50. Spine 8 thick at Y -8..0, flush with
the 8 mm tray front wall. Jaws reach 98 mm (`throat = pocket_w + riser_t`). Lower jaw Z -8.5..-1.5,
top jaw Z 33..43. Opening between the TPU faces: 23.5 mm.

**Tray.** Hangs under the clamp on front and back riser posts (50 x 8 each, X 0..50). Tray X 0..172,
both ends open; pocket Y 0..90; floor Z -32.5..-28.5; walls 10 high. Camera window X 83..168,
Y 4..56 with a 2.5 mm chamfer flare underneath. Finger notch in the front wall at X 100. Phone lies
screen up, camera end to the right; cameras land at the front-right.

**Screws.** 3 x M4 x 20 **socket cap** screws (round head Ø6.9 x 4, 3 mm hex socket), triangle layout
at (X10,Y15), (X40,Y15), (X25,Y82). M4 heat-set inserts (6 OD x 6) go in from the top: hole Ø5.6,
6.5 deep, Ø4.5 clearance below.

**Captive pad.** An M4 nut (7 AF, 3.2 thick) is locked flush on each screw tip with blue threadlocker.
The pad has T-slots open at its left end (channel 8.4 wide x 3.6 tall, neck 4.6, lip 1.5); it slides
in from the clamp's left end onto the nuts. Backing the screws out lifts the pad by the lip.

**Shelf range with M4 x 20.** Head underside sits 5.3 mm above the top jaw at rest (`cap_z0` = 48.3),
so the clamp grips shelves about **18.2-23 mm**. M4 x 25 reaches about 13 mm.

**TPU liners.** Dovetail grooves 6 wide at the opening, 9 at the bottom, 2.5 deep, blind 2 mm before
the right end, slide in from the left. Lower jaw grooves at Y 25 and 73; pad grooves at Y 26 and 71
(2.3 mm from the nut slots). Rails parallel to the groove walls: 0.26 side clearance, 0.3 at tip and
end stop. Dovetail geometry is fixed numbers (not parametric) except the X extents.

**Mat.** 0.3 gap to the walls, camera hole = window + 1 mm. Snap pins at (10,10), (10,80), (76,10),
(160,80): shank Ø3.0 in a Ø3.2 hole, conical head Ø4.0 -> 2.6 x 1.4 long seating in a Ø5 x 1.5
counterbore under the floor. Pin positions are fixed numbers.

**Thumb caps.** Ø20 x 7 knob with 8 scallops (fixed: r2.2 at radius 10.6 - not parametric). Inside:
a round pocket Ø7.0 (`head_d + head_fit`) x 4 deep holding the whole head, plus a printed hex key
2.9 AF (`key_af - key_fit`) x 2 long in the pocket ceiling that enters the screw's socket and carries
the torque. Cap underside is level with the head underside, so screw travel is unchanged. Glue the cap
on (CA or epoxy) so it does not pull off when loosening. Chamfers: top 0.8, bottom outer 0.5, pocket
lead-in 0.4, key tip 0.3. Verify the key fits the real screw socket on one test print.

**Edge treatments on the mount.** Tray corners r4 (right end), lower jaw root r1, top jaw root r2,
clamp outer rounds r2, jaw lead-in chamfers 1.5, tray rim r0.8, pad edges r1, camera window flare 2.5.

## Printing (short)

- Mount body and pad: ABS-GF, hardened nozzle, enclosure, 4-5 walls, 35-40 % gyroid. Mount body
  stands on its flat left end (X = 0), brim; no supports except possibly the 52 mm camera-window bridge.
- Pad: flat, dovetail side down.
- TPU 95A parts: flat face down, rails / pins up, 100 % infill, slow.
- Thumb caps: top face on the bed (pocket up), 100 % infill or 6 walls.

## Assembly order (short)

1. Test-fit TPU liners. 2. Heat-set inserts from the top. 3. Glue thumb caps onto the screw heads;
thread screws down through the inserts; lock a nut flush on each tip with threadlocker. 4. Pad TPU
onto the pad. 5. Slide the pad in from the left onto the nuts until it stops. 6. Jaw TPU into the
lower jaw. 7. Snap the mat into the tray. Mount: caps up, slide onto the shelf, tighten front pair
then back, snug only.

## Notes for continuing in ScanRuler

- Start with `scanruler_status`; if no page, `scanruler_open`, then `project_load` the `.scanruler` file.
- Take `history_checkpoint` before a run of changes.
- After changes: `reverse_body_check { roundTrip: true }`, re-export STLs (`reverse_export_stl` per
  body), `reverse_export_step { verify: true }`, `project_save`.
- `view_render` will not overwrite an existing file: delete the old PNG first.
- In one `reverse_feature_update { features: [...] }` call, fillet picks are read on the pre-update
  body - update fillet edges in a separate call after the rebuild.
- Sketch-solver noise (~1e-9 mm) once broke the "Clamp outer rounds" fillet; writing the sketch with
  exact coordinates fixed it.
- A body can end up hidden; `reverse_body_set { visible: true }` brings it back.
- The thumb-cap extrude makes 3 separate solids; a chamfer named on "Thumb caps" with a `where` query
  becomes one chamfer per solid.
- Python for the guide: run without `-I` (reportlab and PIL are user-installed). `pdfinfo` /
  `pdftoppm` are available; pypdf is not.
